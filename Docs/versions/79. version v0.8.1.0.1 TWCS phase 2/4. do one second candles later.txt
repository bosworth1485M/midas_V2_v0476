Short answer: yes, it absolutely can make sense – later. And the way you’ve framed it (“later”) is exactly how I’d play it.

Let me break it into the trade-offs and where it fits in your roadmap.

1. Why 1-second candles are valuable (later)

Adding 1-second bars around trades gives you things 1-minute bars will never show:

Micro fakeouts & stalls
You can see:

Does price grind into your entry then instantly reverse?

Are there weird “spike then fade” patterns inside a 1-minute candle?

Precision on “was this a good entry?”
Maybe the minute looks fine, but the 1-second view shows:

Entry was at the very top of a micro spike.

There was zero follow-through at the micro level.

Slippage and spreads in context
With 1s bars you can see:

The micro range and volatility at the exact time you sent the order.

Whether you got slipped in a genuine spike or just poor liquidity.

Better reasoning about gates
Micro patterns can tell you things like:

“Strong momentum continuation inside the candle” vs

“Choppy up/down micro moves” (bad environment).

That’s exactly the sort of thing the “most profitable Cameron projects” look at when they talk about microstructure.

2. Why it makes sense to delay them

There are also real costs and complexity:

Data volume & storage
Full-day 1s data per symbol is big. Even with windows-only (entry/exit), you’re still hitting the API and storing more JSON/PNG.

More moving parts to test
1s data brings:

More parsing, alignment, off-by-one issues.

More failure modes (missing seconds, partial windows, etc.).

Danger of “premature micro-optimization”
If the minute-level TWCS, DB, and analysis layer aren’t stable, 1s can easily become a distraction. It’s very easy to drown in micro noise before the macro pipeline is solid.

Given where you are:

Phase 1: structure & meta → done

Phase 2: minute candles + basic indicators → now

Phase 3+: PNG rendering & DB → soon

…it’s much cleaner to:

Get minute-level TWCS fully working and trusted.

Build the DB & analysis loop on minutes.

Then add 1s windows as an extra channel of information.

3. The “right” way to add 1-second candles (later)

When you do add them, I’d stick to this philosophy:

Windows only, not full-day 1s dumps

For each trade, 60–120 seconds before entry and exit is plenty.

Attach them as candles_1s in the same TWCS JSON.

Use them as diagnostics, not as primary triggers (at first)
Phase 1 usage:

Visual inspection (PNG plots).

Post-trade analysis.

Identifying micro patterns that correlate with good/bad trades.

Only later, promote successful micro patterns into actual gates
e.g.,

“Avoid entries where 1s volatility explodes right before the trigger.”

“Prefer entries with clean 1s staircase patterns after the gate.”

That keeps the roadmap clean and respects your measurable profitability rule: microstructure filters should be proven beneficial before they get wired into live logic.
